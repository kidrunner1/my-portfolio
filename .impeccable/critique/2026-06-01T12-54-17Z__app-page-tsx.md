## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment:** The page does not immediately look like a generic AI template. It has a real identity: deep navy, teal action, globe proof object, Thai/English voice, and production-minded copy. The risk is not "bad AI slop"; the risk is sameness from repeated cards, repeated tracked labels, and similar section cadence.

**Deterministic scan:** The detector found 1 warning in `app/page.tsx`: `gray-on-color` at line 84, `text-slate-950 on bg-teal-400`. I treat this as a likely false positive because dark slate text on teal CTA is high-contrast and fits the design system.

**Visual overlays:** Browser overlay was not available in this session. Node/browser automation failed with a Windows sandbox kernel error, so no user-visible detector overlay was injected.

## Overall Impression

The homepage is already far above a raw portfolio template. It communicates "front-end developer who cares about UX and production" quickly. The single biggest opportunity is to make the first viewport and selected work section prove the claim more decisively: less ambient decoration, more specific evidence.

## What's Working

1. **The hero has a strong brand object.** `LazyGlobe` gives the page a memorable technical/global motif instead of relying only on text.
2. **The CTA pair is clear.** Portfolio and Contact are the right primary actions for HR, companies, freelance clients, and startups.
3. **The visual system is cohesive.** Dark navy, teal, rose, translucent cards, 8px radius, and generous Thai line-height are consistent with `DESIGN.md`.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Hero proof panels compete with the headline and globe

**Why it matters:** In the first viewport, users see the badge, H1, paragraph, two CTAs, three stats, three floating proof panels, and the globe. That is a lot of simultaneous scanning for a recruiter or client with limited time.

**Fix:** Keep the globe as the hero proof object, but reduce the floating panels to one strong statement or convert them into a cleaner orbit/status strip. Let the H1 and CTA own the left side; let one proof layer support the globe.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout app/page.tsx hero`

### [P1] Selected Work lacks immediate visual proof

**Why it matters:** The homepage says the work is polished, but the selected project cards are text-only summaries. For HR and clients, screenshots or a concrete outcome create trust faster than another paragraph.

**Fix:** Add one thumbnail, mini screenshot strip, metric, or compact case-study preview per featured project. The homepage should show proof before asking users to click Portfolio.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable polish app/page.tsx selected work`

### [P2] Section rhythm risks becoming template-like

**Why it matters:** `Toolset`, `How I Work`, and `Selected Work` all rely on kicker label + heading + card/grid grammar. It is consistent, but the PRODUCT.md explicitly says the site should not feel like a generic template.

**Fix:** Give each section a different job and composition: Toolset as grouped capability bands, How I Work as a process timeline, Selected Work as proof-led case previews.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable bolder app/page.tsx sections`

### [P2] Toolset chips are accurate but low-signal

**Why it matters:** Eight chips tell users what tools exist, but not how strong or relevant each tool is. Recruiters and clients need a faster read of "what can this person do for me?"

**Fix:** Group chips into Build / UX / Ship or Front-End / Product UI / Delivery. Add short supporting context instead of a flat list.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify app/page.tsx toolset`

### [P2] The homepage underuses specific outcomes

**Why it matters:** The brand promise is production-ready craft, but several blocks talk in broad qualities like UX, performance, maintainability, and value. Specific outcomes are more persuasive.

**Fix:** Rewrite one or two content blocks around concrete outcomes: faster scanning, completed live demos, dashboard clarity, authentication/cart/orders in KICKS VAULT, or build/lint/performance discipline.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify app/page.tsx copy`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (First-Timer):** The role and actions are clear, but the hero has many simultaneous proof elements. Jordan may understand "Front-End Developer" quickly, then slow down deciding whether to inspect stats, floating panels, toolset, or selected work.

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User):** The globe placeholder and reduced-motion behavior are good, but the stacked hero still has a lot of content before proof of projects. Casey needs a very fast path to one impressive project and one contact action.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent User):** CTAs have visible focus styles, and semantic headings are present. Risk areas are decorative globe/floating proof text becoming extra screen-reader noise and any future visual-only route/globe meaning not having text equivalents.

**Maya (HR / Hiring Reviewer):** Maya wants role, stack, credibility, and strongest proof in under 30 seconds. The page gives role and stack quickly, but strongest proof is still one click away in Portfolio.

## Minor Observations

- The "Available for Front-End and Web App projects" badge is useful; it could be stronger if paired with availability or work mode only once.
- `How I Work` is credible, but it repeats values already implied by hero copy. It can become more concrete.
- The detector warning on the primary CTA should not drive a change unless a real contrast check fails.
- Repeated uppercase tracked section labels should stay limited. The design system already calls this out.

## Questions to Consider

- What would the homepage look like if the first viewport had only one proof object and one proof sentence?
- Which single project should HR or clients remember after 10 seconds?
- Can the Toolset section show capability levels or outcomes instead of technology inventory?
